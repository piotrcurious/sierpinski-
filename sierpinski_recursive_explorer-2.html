One rule in both dimensions

Parent simplex: [x₁, x₂, x₃, …, xₙ₊₁]
Tagged edge: x₁ → x₂
Midpoint: m = (x₁ + x₂)/2

Left child: [x₁, x₃, x₄, …, m]
Right child: [x₃, x₂, x₄, …, m]

Child tags: x₁ → x₃ → x₂

In 2D, the parent is a triangle and each split produces two triangles. In 3D, it is a tetrahedron and each split produces two tetrahedra sharing the face [x₃, x₄, m]. The child volumes are exactly half the parent volume.

Exact match to the four planar maps

K = [A, B, C]
A=(0,0), B=(1,0), C=(½,½)

First split on AB at M=(½,0):
[A,C,M] · [C,B,M]

Second split on AC and CB:
[A,M,N] · [M,C,N]
[C,M,P] · [M,B,P]

N=(¼,¼), P=(¾,¼)

At binary depth 2 these are exactly S₀(K), S₁(K), S₂(K), S₃(K), in the same traversal order as the four-map IFS. This is the bridge from the previous derivation to the 3D rule.

Why the 3D limit fills volume

1. Each split partitions a tetrahedron into two half-volume tetrahedra.
2. The child tags join at x₃, so consecutive path pieces meet.
3. Barycentric matrices for the left and right children are row-stochastic. Exhausting all 2⁸ = 256 products of length 8 gives max Dobrushin coefficient δ = ½.
4. Since diam(P·T) ≤ δ(P) diam(T), every 8 levels each cell diameter is at most half its ancestor's.
5. Nested cells converge to points; because the cells partition the parent at every level, every point is reached. The same estimate gives a conservative Hölder exponent 1/8 for the parameterized map.

Not a cube curve: the filled domain is a tetrahedron (or two tetrahedra sharing a face in the paired mode). This is a dimensional extension of the tagged-edge recursion selected by the 2D derivation; the planar rules alone do not uniquely force it. The four planar similarity maps are not being treated as four isotropic 3D maps.

The tetrahedra become increasingly skewed; the standard 1/3-Hölder/locality behavior is lost. The 8-level contraction certificate still gives a weaker 1/8-Hölder bound. It is a conservative bound, not an optimal exponent.

Navigation

In 3D, drag to orbit and use the wheel or pinch to zoom. The section slider cuts through the interior along a horizontal plane. Scrub t to select a leaf cell and its binary address. The bright edge pair is that cell's tagged entry and exit.
