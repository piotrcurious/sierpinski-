For the currently selected dimension, every binary product of the barycentric child matrices is enumerated exactly with integer arithmetic. The maximum Dobrushin coefficient bounds the Euclidean diameter of the whole descendant simplex through a convex-combination coupling inequality.

The coefficient bound controls the Euclidean diameter of the entire simplex via convex combinations of its vertices.

The displayed exponent is a guaranteed, conservative bound, not an optimality claim. Any Hölder map from an interval onto a region with positive d-dimensional volume must have exponent at most 1/d.

This is not a shape-regularity guarantee. Tetrahedra can become sliver-like; shrinking diameter, not bounded aspect ratio, is the property used for convergence.
