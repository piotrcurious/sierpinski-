Exact recursive rule

K = conv{(0,0), (1,0), (1/2,1/2)}

S₀(x,y) = (x/2, y/2)
S₁(x,y) = (1/2 − y/2, x/2)
S₂(x,y) = (1/2 + y/2, 1/2 − x/2)
S₃(x,y) = (1/2 + x/2, y/2)

Ports:
(0,0) → (1/2,0) → (1/2,1/2)
→ (1/2,0) → (1,0)
The 2D maps tile K exactly. Every child has scale 1/2, and adjacent endpoint ports coincide.

Direct 3D lift

P = (0,0,0), Q = (1,0,1)

T₀(x,y,z) = (x/2, y/2, z/2)
T₁(x,y,z) = (1/2−y/2, x/2, 1/2+z/2)
T₂(x,y,z) = (1/2+y/2, 1/2−x/2, 1−z/2)
T₃(x,y,z) = (1/2+x/2, y/2, 1/2+z/2)
Endpoint checks:
T₀(Q) = T₁(P) = (1/2,0,1/2)
T₁(Q) = T₂(P) = (1/2,1/2,1)
T₂(Q) = T₃(P) = (1/2,0,1/2)
T₀(P)=P; T₃(Q)=Q

What this proves: a continuous 3D self-similar lift whose XY projection is exactly the original triangular curve. Z is generated recursively by the child maps; it is not traversal time or an extrusion.

What it does not prove: volume filling. Four isotropic half-scale children have similarity dimension log(4)/log(2) = 2. Their 3D cell volume totals half the parent volume at each refinement. The lift is a fractal surface-like set in 3D, not a space-filling volume curve.

Square axiom root

A(x,y) = (y, −x)
B(x,y) = (−y, x−1)

A(P₂) = (0,0), A(Q₂) = (0,−1)
B(P₂) = (0,−1), B(Q₂) = (0,0)

3D root maps:
A₃(x,y,z) = (y, −x, z/2)
B₃(x,y,z) = (−y, x−1, z/2+1/2)
A₃(Q) = B₃(P) = (0,−1,1/2)

The axiom joins two rotated triangular fragments along their common diagonal. In the 3D view, their copies occupy successive Z slabs, meeting at z = 1/2. The XY projection remains the square-filling construction. The finite unit connector strokes are omitted; they vanish under the normalization dₙ = 4(2ⁿ−1).

How to navigate

In 3D, drag to orbit and scroll to zoom. Scrub t to inspect the address and the current terminal cell. Auto-rotate can be disabled. The highlighted wireframe is the selected recursive cell.

In 2D, the level-n triangles cover K. In 3D, the lifted level-n cells occupy a fraction 2−n of the parent triangular prism.
