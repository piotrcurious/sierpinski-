Why this is volumetric

At each level: 4 child prisms, each with volume 1/4 of its parent. Two axes halve; the held axis is chosen from the entry/exit state.

Two axes are bisected; the third is retained. Which axis stays whole is selected from the current entry/exit corner pair. The four cells follow the square curve’s 2×2 cycle; their subcurves meet at shared-face junction corners.

4⁴ = 256 prisms · volume per prism = 1/256
sideᵢ(n) = 2−(n−uᵢ(n)) · max(uᵢ) − min(uᵢ) ≤ 2 at 4-level boundaries

A minimax finite-state planner chooses the three junction corners and the held axis. At every four-level boundary, the counts of uncut axes differ by at most two. Since each axis is therefore cut at a positive asymptotic rate, the largest nested prism diameter tends to zero. The partitions remain exact covers of the cube, so their limit is a continuous curve onto the entire volume.

This is a new quaternary, graph-directed Peano construction—not a coordinate embedding of the original 2D drawing. Its recursive branching and endpoint order are inherited; its spatial contraction maps are newly derived.
